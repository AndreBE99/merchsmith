Recommended range

Nothing gets recommended because someone paid for it.

The Recommended range is the shortlist a distributor can trade from on day one without checking anything themselves. That only works if the bar is real, so every product on it has been through the same assessment: seen, handled, branded, costed and timed.

This page sets out how that assessment works, what it costs a supplier, and the terms that come with a placement.

01

The five things we assess

Every product is scored against the same five. A product can be excellent on four and still not make the range — the weakest one decides it, because it is the one that will fail a customer.

Sustainability

Claims are checked against documents, not marketing copy. We want to know what it is made of, who made it, and what happens at the end of its life.

  • Certification evidence, not logos
  • Recycled content verified at source
  • Factory audit status
  • Packaging and end-of-life route

Brandability

A product only earns its place if a logo looks right on it. We brand a sample before deciding, because print area on a spec sheet rarely matches print area in the hand.

  • Print area measured, not quoted
  • Surface tested per method
  • Colour behaviour on the material
  • Origination cost proportionate

Uniqueness

The range is a shortlist, not a catalogue. If three suppliers offer near-identical stock we take the best one, and we keep space for products that give a distributor something to say.

  • Judged against what is already in
  • Design or function worth noting
  • Not a generic import rebadged

Price

Not the cheapest — the right price for what it is. We check that the cost holds at real quantities and that a distributor can make a fair margin without the end price looking silly.

  • Breaks tested at working volumes
  • Decoration and setup included
  • Margin viable at both ends
  • Benchmarked against the category

Lead time

A quoted lead time is a claim until it has survived a real order. We hold suppliers to the figure they give us, and the range reflects what actually happens, not what was promised.

  • Verified against delivery history
  • Stock depth and restock cadence
  • Branding time counted in
  • Behaviour under a rush reviewed
02

How a product gets there

The order matters. We meet the people who make it before we assess the product, because most of what goes wrong later is a factory problem rather than a product one.

1

We meet the manufacturer

In person wherever we can, at the factory where we can get there. We want to see the line, understand capacity, and know who we call when an order is at risk. A supplier we cannot reach on a bad day does not belong in a range other people trade from.

Merchsmith product team
2

We use the product

Samples go into real use — carried, washed, charged, dropped, refilled. Weeks, not minutes. Most rejections happen here, and usually for something that no specification would have told us.

Merchsmith product team
3

We brand it ourselves

Every candidate is decorated before a decision, in the methods a customer would actually ask for. It tells us what the print area really is, how the surface takes ink, and whether the result is something we would be happy to put a client's logo on.

Merchsmith product team
4

We cost it honestly

Cost price, decoration, setup, carriage and the breaks that matter at working volumes. We model what a distributor would sell it for and check the margin stands up at both ends.

5

We apply the experience

Years of orders tell you things testing cannot: which categories generate complaints, which factories slip in Q4, which products look superb and are returned anyway. That history is the last filter, and it overrules a good score.

6

The supplier approves the placement

If a product passes, we propose it to the supplier with its tier and terms — and, from Phase 2, its monthly charge. Nothing appears in any distributor's store until they accept.

03

The two tiers

Both tiers clear the same assessment. The difference is placement, not standard: Featured products surface first in search and are eligible for the homepage and best-seller positions.

Recommended

Included in the Recommended range that every distributor can trade from by default.

  • Appears in the default store range
  • Full product data maintained by Merchsmith
  • Ranked normally in search and category pages
Featured

Everything above, plus priority placement across the storefront.

  • Sorts above standard products in listings
  • Eligible for homepage and best-seller placements
  • Carries a Featured badge on the product card

Placement is free during MVP. Recommended and Featured are a curation flag we set to influence store visibility — no supplier is charged. The fees below are a Phase 2 model, built into the platform and switched on later. Until then, everything described here runs without a charge, so suppliers can see exactly how it will work before it costs anything.

04

Terms of a placement

Short, and the same for everyone.

Approval first Merchsmith proposes a placement with its tier and charge. It goes live only once the supplier accepts, and a supplier may decline without affecting anything else in their account.
Charged in whole months Placement is billed by the month, never pro-rated: a month started is a month charged. Withdrawing a product costs one further whole month — pull it half way through its third month and four months are charged. There is no term beyond that.
Monthly, by direct debit Fees are collected monthly by Stripe Direct Debit against the mandate on the supplier's account. One invoice per month covering every placement, itemised by product.
Leave whenever A supplier can withdraw a product at any time, and Merchsmith can remove one at any time if it stops meeting the standard. Either way the closing whole month applies and nothing further is charged.
Changes are published Every addition, tier change and removal is dated and written to a change log that suppliers and distributors can both see. A distributor whose store loses a product is told, so they can add it back to their own range if they want to keep selling it.
