Merchsmith — the platform in full

A white-labelled merchandise procurement platform: Merchsmith operates it, a distributor runs it for their customers, with suppliers feeding products and producing behind the scenes. This brief captures how the pieces fit together — what's built, what's concepted, and what's next — as a shared reference for the development team.

1Who uses it

Merchsmith operates the platform; one distributor account sits at the hub of each install. Customers and suppliers live underneath it and never see each other. Role drives what every screen shows — switched in the prototype via the floating "Viewing as" pill.

Platform Admin Operator

Merchsmith's console: onboards distributors and suppliers, imports and normalises supplier feeds, curates the master product database into the recommended range (with listing/promotion fees and supplier invoicing), and manages store layout, platform branding and data backup.

Distributor Hub

Runs the platform under their own co-brand and colourway. Manages customers, suppliers, pricing/markup, lead times, their store range (recommended / edited / bespoke), the store homepage content, and every project via the master Projects table. Sees cost, margin & profit.

Customer Buyer

Browses the store, builds projects (Create Project wizard + brief), approves visuals/proofs & prices, orders samples, picks delivery dates, reviews products for their organisation. Sees sell price only. Can have teams (Marketing, HR…) ordering separately.

Supplier Producer

Product (blanks) or branding (decoration). Provides the product feed (API / CSV / manual), replies to stock / delivery / quote enquiries, confirms critical delivery dates, receives orders, uploads proofs for the distributor to check.

3The surfaces

Two navigation models coexist: a left account sidebar and the Store top-nav. Everything below is clickable in the prototype — see the Developer Index for the full page list with per-page change history.

Store Built

Reads the shared catalogue (real imported feeds), filtered by the distributor's range. Data-driven top nav and departments with faceted filters (sub-category, price, colour, brand, eco).

  • Homepage is distributor-editable (Store Settings): show/hide/duplicate/reorder sections, hand-picked Best Sellers, trust badges, brand marquee, discover tiles, customer quotes, sustainability band.
  • Product page: gallery with zoom + swatch-linked images, USP story blocks, org-only reviews, "You might also like", price breaks & lead time, Add to Project. Distributor sees the full canonical data pane + price matrix.
Projects Built

The heart of the workflow, at two levels:

  • Project workspace — product table with the four-stage modal, brief control panel, comments, supplier tags, dual Supplier/Customer delivery + order-by dates, PDF project sheets.
  • Master Projects table (distributor) — every project for every customer with expandable product rows (the same engine embedded), KPI cards, and combinable filters: project status, product status, order-by windows (Overdue / Today / Tomorrow / This week), stage breakdown (who owes the next action), collaborator + supplier.
Platform Admin console Built

Supplier onboarding (guided wizard / invite / quick add) with connect-time field mapping; the multi-feed import wizard (adapter framework, value mapping, preview-in-store); master product database (sortable, bulk actions, sub-category + brand tagging); recommended range with placement tiers, fees and supplier invoicing; store layout manager; platform branding; data backup + blank go-live mode.

Brand Assets Built

The customer's DAM: guidelines, logos, colours (Pantone refs), fonts, images, product artwork. Selections flow into the project brief and the branding builder; uploaded artwork surfaces against matching brief lines.

Basket & Orders Built

Orders + sample orders with per-line delivery and addresses; order history and status tracking. Duplicate products combine to reach higher quantity-break rates.

Finance / checkout Next

Billing address, payment terms (at order vs 30 days), payment method (card, BACS, Apple/Google Pay), credit limit, payment deadline warnings, Order Reference / PO upload. Nav placeholders exist (Finance → Invoices / Credit Limit); the pages are the next build alongside the backend (Stripe + Xero).

5Cross-cutting threads

Sustainability

Phase 1 ships a USP strip on product pages; the full scored breakdown (Origin, Materials, Longevity, End of Life, Certifications) is Phase 2, gated behind a platform flag. Eco scores already import from real feeds and filter in store.

Money & terms

Distributor markup bands, per-customer discounts & credit limits, VAT throughout, PO handling. Plus the platform's own commercial model: recommended-range listing + promotion fees invoiced to suppliers monthly.

Roles & visibility

Cost/margin/profit is distributor-only; the customer sees sell price & savings. Data overrides and supplier agreements are distributor-only. Every surface flips by role, including the two project tables.

6Where it goes next

Going live (the big one) — a real backend: live supplier API pulls, Stripe payments, Xero invoicing, shared persistence (today everything is localStorage, per-browser, with backup/restore as the seatbelt).
Finance pages — Invoices + Credit Limit are nav placeholders; build alongside checkout.
Hand-over to account manager — an explicit lifecycle stage: customer builds the brief + candidates, then hands the project to their AM; collaborators formally attach at that moment.
Finder (Phase 2) — AI assistant that suggests store products against a brief line (type, qty, target price, colours, eco, deadline), ranked with rationale.
Permissions gating — roles are documented (e.g. customer "Studio" edits Brand Assets) but not yet enforced in the UI.

7The product feed — proven on real data

The original open question ("can the store run on a real feed?") is answered: yes, end to end, minus only the live pull.

  • Real feeds imported: PF Concept (2,000+ products across 4 joined JSON feeds — price, print, stock, product), Xoopar (223 products from an Akeneo PIM export, EUR→GBP), Bio Labs (411-row WooCommerce CSV). All flow through the adapter framework → field/value mapping → product database → recommended range → store.
  • The data contract is proven: decoration cost books, price breaks, stock, variants, images, certifications and custom fields all round-trip from feed to store product page.
  • The remaining gap is infrastructure, not design: this prototype can't make live authenticated API calls — production points the same import pipeline at the real endpoints on a schedule. That's part of the going-live backend phase.
