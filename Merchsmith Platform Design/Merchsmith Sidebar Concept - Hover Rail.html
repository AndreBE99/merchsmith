Navigation concept

The hover rail

Now live across the platform. The rail stays narrow permanently and every section answers on hover with a panel of its destinations. This page is kept as the rationale for the change; the rail on the left is the shipped one.

Why it might be better

The page never moves

Nothing expands in place, so the content area keeps a fixed width. Wide tables — the master projects view especially — stop reflowing every time someone opens the menu.

Two levels, one gesture

Today a collapsed icon has to be clicked to expand, then clicked again to reach a sub-page. Here the whole section is visible after one hover, so any destination is a single click.

Always the same

Every icon opens a panel, including single-destination ones. Nothing is hidden behind "which icons expand and which just navigate".

What it gives up

Labels are gone at rest. Icons alone carry the navigation, which is fine for daily users and harder for new ones — the panel header names the section, but only once you are already hovering. Hover does not exist on touch, so tablets need the tap-to-open fallback (built in here). And a panel that appears without being asked for can feel twitchy, so the open is delayed slightly and a hidden bridge spans the gap, letting the pointer cut the corner without the panel vanishing.

Details worth noticing

  • The active section keeps a tinted icon and a marker on the rail edge, so the current location is readable with no labels.
  • Counts sit in the panel — open projects, unread chats — rather than needing a second badge system.
  • Keyboard works: tab to an icon and the panel opens on focus; Escape closes it.
  • Click the icon itself to go straight to the section's main page without choosing from the panel.
