2 · Who looks for what

The same search bar works for everyone, but what it searches, and in what order, depends on who is signed in. These are the jobs each user type comes to search for. Agreed

CustomerBuyer, approver, finance, viewer
  • A product to buy: “bamboo pen”, “navy tote”, “under £5”
  • Their project or a product in it
  • “Where’s my order?”: order ref or product name
  • An invoice to pay, a proof to approve
  • A logo or asset in Brand Assets
  • Their account manager or a colleague
Never sees: cost, margin, suppliers, other customers.
DistributorAdmin, account manager, studio, production, finance
  • A customer, then everything for them
  • An order, PO, quote, invoice or credit note by number
  • A product by name, SKU or supplier code (store + full catalogue)
  • A supplier’s whole product range, to find an alternative
  • A supplier and their contacts
  • A person: name, email, phone (caller lookup)
  • A setting: “markup”, “footer”, “lead times”
Their own customers rank first for account managers (Q5); Finance sees invoices first, Studio sees proofs first.
SupplierAdmin, contact
  • A PO by number, or the distributor’s PO ref
  • Their own product by name or code
  • Blank stock coming in / going out
  • A distributor contact
Never sees: end-customer names, sell prices, other suppliers.
Platform admin (Merchsmith)Owner, admin, support, billing
  • A distributor, supplier or any user by email (support)
  • A product in the master catalogue, mapping issues
  • A subscription invoice
  • Any distributor’s projects and orders (always searchable, Q6)
Searches across every organisation; each result names its organisation.

3 · Header search: how it behaves

One search for the whole platform, opened from the header bar or the keyboard. Results drop down under the header bar as you type (full screen on phones), grouped by type, with the most likely one first. Agreed (Q1)

OpeningClick the bar, press / or Ctrl K (⌘ K on Mac) from any page. On phones, a search icon opens it full screen.
Before typingRecent searches, recently opened items (last 5), and 3–4 suggestions for the role: customer “Track an order”, “Approve proofs (2)”; distributor “Orders due this week”, “Unpaid invoices”.
While typingResults from the first character for refs and the second for words. Up to 3 per group, “See all 14 orders” under each. One top result when a match is clear, ready for Enter.
Exact references jumpA full order, PO, quote, invoice or SKU number goes straight to the record on Enter: MS-1054, PO-1054-X1, Q-261015-118, INV-2041, LM-CABLE-01.
NarrowingChips under the box: All · Products · Supplier ranges · Projects · Orders · People… Hidden typed shortcuts also work for power users: in:orders, status:overdue, customer:citrix, supplier:bagworks; typing one switches the matching chip on so it’s clear what happened (Q3). On a list page the chip starts on that list (“Search orders”), with All one tap away.
Actions (everyone, Q4)Typing a verb offers it, limited to what that person can do: customers “new project”, “invite a colleague”, “pay invoice”; distributors “add customer”, “settings markup”; suppliers “upload invoice”; admins “invite distributor”.
Keyboard↑ ↓ move, Enter opens, Ctrl Enter opens in a new tab, Tab moves between chips, Esc closes.
Full results pageEnter with no top result, or “See all”, opens a results page with filters down the side (type, organisation, status, date) and the query in the URL so it can be shared.

5 · Matching

  • Ignores case, accents, punctuation and spaces in refs: “ms1054”, “MS 1054” and “1054” all find MS-1054.
  • Matches the start of any word: “trav” finds Tech Travel Bag. Every word must match, in any order.
  • Allows one typo in words of 5+ letters: “notbook” finds notebook. Says “Showing results for notebook”.
  • Merch synonyms from one fixed Merchsmith list (Q7): tote ↔ bag ↔ shopper, mug ↔ cup, pen ↔ biro, hoodie ↔ sweatshirt, eco ↔ sustainable ↔ recycled.
  • Colour words use the platform colour map: “navy” also finds Dark Blue and Midnight.
  • Plurals and simple endings: bags → bag, printed → print.
  • Price and quantity words in the store: “under £5”, “500 pens” (shows the price at 500).

Ranking

  1. Exact reference or SKU match.
  2. Things that are yours and live: your open projects and orders, your own customers for account managers (all customers stay searchable, Q5), anything awaiting your action.
  3. Name matches before description matches; whole words before word starts.
  4. Recently opened or updated.
  5. Group order follows the role: customers see Products → Projects → Orders; Finance sees Documents first; suppliers see POs first.

No results: say what was searched and where, offer the nearest spelling, “Search everywhere” if a chip was on, and for customers in the store, “Ask your account manager to find it”, which starts a chat with the search text.

6 · List search boxes: one pattern everywhere

The search boxes on lists (S03–S08) stay, because filtering the table you are looking at is a different job from finding anything anywhere. They should all behave the same. Agreed

Wording“Search orders by ref, customer or product”: the noun, then what it matches.
FeedbackFilters as you type; matches highlighted; count updates (“6 of 41 orders”); × to clear; Esc clears.
RememberedThe query goes in the URL (#q=tote), so Back and shared links keep it; it combines with the list’s filters.
Same matchingUses the rules in section 5, so “1054” works the same in the Orders table and the header.
Empty“No orders match ‘tote’. Clear filters · Search everywhere →”.
Fix firstOrganisations → Customers and Suppliers search boxes, which appear unwired.

7 · Store search (shopping)

In the store, the header search behaves like a shop’s: products first, with images. Agreed

  • Suggestions: matching departments and sub-categories (“Bags › Totes”), then products with image and “from” price, then the distributor’s popular searches.
  • Your projects and orders: a small group under the products (up to 3), so a customer can still jump to a project or order from the store (Q2).
  • Results page: the category grid and filters (price, colour, brand, eco, branding method, lead time) with the query as a removable chip; sort by relevance, price, newest, fastest lead time.
  • Only the distributor’s range: customers never see catalogue products outside the store range. Distributor previewing the store sees what customers see, with an “Also in the catalogue (12)” link.
  • Promoted products: Spotlight / Featured products rank first when they match.
  • From a project: “Add product” in a project opens the same search with “Add to project” on each result.
  • Search insights (Q8): Distributor Settings → Store settings shows top searches, searches with no results (“12 customers searched ‘lanyard’”) and searches with no clicks, over the last 30 / 90 days, with “Find in catalogue” to add matching products to the range.

8 · Permissions and privacy

  • Search never reveals something you could not open. Results are filtered by the same rules as the pages, before counting, so counts never leak (“3 orders” must not include hidden ones). Agreed principle
  • Customers: their own organisation only; no cost, margin or supplier names in matches or snippets.
  • Suppliers: only POs and products addressed to them; end-customer names are never matched or shown (blank-stock suppliers included).
  • Distributor roles all see all customers; account managers get their own ranked first (Q5).
  • Platform admins can search every distributor’s projects and orders (Q6). Each result names the distributor.
  • Recent searches are stored per person and can be cleared.

9 · Suggested build order

Step 1Search engine + header searchOne shared matcher (section 5) and the header dropdown for projects, orders, documents, products, people, pages. Exact-ref jump, chips, hidden shortcuts, actions.
Step 2Store searchSuggestions, results page with filters, synonyms and colour words, supplier ranges, search insights.
Step 3List boxesEvery list search moved onto the shared matcher and pattern (section 6).
Step 4Full results page + messagesResults page with side filters; chats, emails, calls and transcripts.

All four steps are built in the prototype (search.js, 15 Oct). In the prototype, search runs in the browser over the demo data. Going live needs a search index on the backend that applies the same permission rules.

Decisions

Agreed 15 Oct 2026.

Q1 Where results appear. Dropdown under the header bar; full screen on phones.
Q2 Store header search. Products first, then a small “Your projects and orders” group.
Q3 Narrowing. Chips, with typed shortcuts (in:orders, status:overdue) as a hidden extra.
Q4 Actions in search. Yes, for everyone, limited to what each person can do.
Q5 Account managers. All customers searchable; their own ranked first.
Q6 Platform admin. Can always search distributors’ projects and orders.
Q7 Synonyms. One fixed Merchsmith list.
Q8 Search insights. Yes, in Store settings.
Q9 Messages. Own group in the header search, built in step 4.
Q10 Supplier ranges. Added: search a supplier’s whole product range, as its own chip (from your feedback).
