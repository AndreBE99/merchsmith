Organisations & users

How accounts, organisations and people fit together on the platform — who owns what, who can see whom, and the roles each person can hold.

Mock-up only — nothing live has changed. For you to react to before we build the Organisations area.

1The hierarchy

Four organisation types. The Distributor is the hub: it owns its Customer organisations (who place orders) and connects to Suppliers (who make & decorate the goods). Each organisation holds its own users.

Operates the platform; provisions distributor accounts. Each distributor runs under their own domain & branding — customers never see “merchsmith.com”.

5Key rules

  • Distributor is the hub. Customer orgs and suppliers exist under a distributor; they don't see each other.
  • Customer isolation. A customer org only ever sees its own projects, prices (sell only) and approvals — never cost, markup or other customers.
  • Suppliers are added by the distributor and can have contacts added inline (as in the delivery/quote flows). A product line may pair a product supplier + a branding supplier. TODO: revisit supplier “add contact / user” permissions for the customer side.
  • One user = one organisation. A person belongs to a single org; the same email can't span orgs.
  • No cross-distributor switching. If a customer works with multiple Merchsmith distributors, each is a separate login — no shared data; they log out of one and into another.
  • The Customer / Distributor view toggle already in the app maps to whether the signed-in user belongs to a customer org or the distributor.
  • Customer sub-teams. A customer org can have teams / departments (e.g. Marketing, HR) that order separately; users belong to a team, and projects/orders can be scoped per team.
  • Suppliers manage their own product feed (API or manual upload / edit of products), receive orders, and upload proofs for the distributor to check — in addition to replying to stock / delivery / quote enquiries.
  • Settings live per org: distributor (markup bands, days-in-lieu, lead times), supplier (lead times, contacts, product feed), customer (standing discount, addresses, teams).
